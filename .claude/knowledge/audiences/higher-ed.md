# Audience: Higher Education

Who CUBE84 sells to in higher education, what job they are doing, and what will make them stop
reading. Written 2026-09-02, replacing `higher ed persona.md`, which was Student-Engagement-only,
carried 26 real named individuals with their employers, and cited a source PDF that is not on
disk.

**Three files defined this audience three different ways.** That is resolved here. The governing
ruling is on record in `higher-ed-dashboard/content/decisions.ts`.

## The rulings that settle it

**2026-06-03, Kiki.** The case-manager ICP was abandoned after 48 connected conversations produced
zero booked meetings. Outbound was repointed at **IT and CTO-level roles**.

**2026-03-31, the BU lead and the data lead.** Include advancement, student success, alumni
management and fundraising titles. **Exclude admissions and enrolment.** On LinkedIn exclude

deliberately include Senior Managers — they are the ones doing the research.

**2026-03-31.** Target smaller institutions first — liberal arts colleges before community
colleges. Smaller institutions move faster.

**2026-06-03, formal.** Qualify on whether the prospect actually runs Salesforce and Education
Cloud, rather than inferring it later. This is not paperwork: it changes lift, price and framing,
and the evidence says the person in front of you often cannot answer it.

## Segment 1 — The platform owner (economic buyer)

Enterprise CRM directors, application services directors, chief data officers, solution and CRM
architects, product owners, engineering managers, executive directors of IT, CTOs and CIOs. At
advancement-led institutions, add the executive director of advancement information services —
**that title produced the single largest higher-ed programme in the CRM, from an unpaid website
form fill.**

**Their job:** keep a sprawling Salesforce estate coherent while every business unit asks for
something different.

**What moves them:** native to the platform they already run — no security review for a new
external tool, no data leaving Salesforce, no new vendor onboarding, no new infrastructure.
Faster time to value. SIS-agnostic integration.

**What they will not do:** buy an application that sits beside the CRM and syncs into it.

## Segment 2 — The practitioner (convener and influencer, not the buyer)

Student affairs and student success: advisors, case managers, success coaches, directors of
student engagement, deans of students, VPs and AVPs of student success, directors of advising.
Instructional designers, who are often the first person a student actually goes to.

**This is the most important correction in this file.** The practitioner is *not* the buyer, and
treating them as one is how 48 conversations produced nothing. But they are not a dead end either
— **five organisations offered to convene the buying group** rather than stalling: bringing a
supervisor, bringing five or six colleagues, offering the introduction to Student Affairs, naming
an IT director who had already told the team to research the platform.

**Asking for the group is the qualification step.** Write for this segment emotionally, qualify
through them structurally.

**A vocabulary rule they police:** success coaching is not academic advising. Two organisations
made the distinction unprompted and one was emphatic about it. Coaching sits outside the advising
org and is measured on persistence and relationship, not registration. Advising-tool positioning
does not reach it.

## Segment 3 — The programme owner (bounded budget)

Directors of federally-funded first-generation and student-support-services programmes. They
report against a bounded roster, so they can adopt a narrow tool without an enterprise decision.

**Read this segment with the provenance warning attached.** It recurs four times in the ledger,
but three of those four came from an outbound list built by targeting exactly those titles. It is
substantially an artifact of the calling list, not observed organic demand.

## What they are already doing on Salesforce

**Most common:** student success and advising · admissions and recruitment · marketing and
communications · case management · enrolled student engagement · event management.
**Also common:** advancement and alumni relations · corporate and external partnerships ·
financial aid · HR and employee services · university housing.
**Emerging:** Agentforce and AI · Data Cloud · ITSM · commerce and storefront.

**Salesforce products in play:** Sales, Service, Marketing, Experience and Education Cloud · EDA ·
CRM Analytics and Tableau · Pardot / Account Engagement · Data Cloud · Agentforce · MuleSoft.

**Systems of record:** Ellucian Banner (most common at small and mid-size) · PeopleSoft / Oracle
Campus Solutions (large publics) · Workday Student (growing) · Canvas, D2L, Brightspace as LMS.

**Governance models**, which decide who can actually sign: *consolidated* (single COE, single org)
· *federated* (multiple COEs, business-unit flexibility) · *confederated* (each unit independent).
Confederated is why the practitioner cannot tell you what the institution runs.

## The buying reality, from the evidence

Each of these is a fingerprint in `highered-signals.md` with an organisation count behind it.

| What happens | Why it happens |
|---|---|
| They cannot state Salesforce status | The CRM is owned by IT, enrolment or advancement — not by them |
| The first contact has no budget line | The budget holder is one to three levels up, in a different reporting chain |
| Budget is refused before evaluation | It is a multi-year planning cycle, not a line item. Ask when the window opens, not for a discount |
| The legacy SIS sets the price | The integration, not the product, is the floor cost |
| They keep a tool they dislike | Dissatisfaction is about permissions and connectivity, not features — so replacement cycles repeat without resolving anything |
| Access tiers come up before features | Who can see what is a procurement gate |
| Graduate cohorts are unserved | Belonging models assume a four-year undergraduate arc; graduate cohorts are short-tenure and terminal |
| The AI budget exists before the use case | The buy decision turns on a credible grounding-and-guardrails answer, not on capability |
| They arrive asking for people, not software | They own the platform and are short of hands. Demand presents as a capacity gap |

## Objection to meaning

| What they say | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| "I'm not the decision maker" | Correct, and they will convene the person who is if asked directly |
| "We don't have budget" | The planning cycle is closed, often for two to three years. Ask when it reopens |
| "We already have something for that" | It does not connect to the CRM or the advising system. That gap is the opening |
| "Send me something I can forward" | A genuine information-gatherer. Send something forwardable, and capture the institution and a work email |
| "Is it built on Salesforce?" | A security-review and infrastructure question wearing a product question's clothes |
| "How do you stop it hallucinating?" | A buy-versus-build decision already leaning buy. Answer with grounding and guardrails, not reassurance |

## Writing rules for this audience

- **Never criticise a platform they have already implemented and defended to their board.** Name
  the mechanism that fails, never the vendor and never their past decision. This is the sharpest
  rule in the market.
- **Establish the problem before introducing the product.**
- No buzzwords, no vague claims. They have been promised a retention fix before.
- Do not explain Navigate360, Starfish, Banner, Ellucian, Canvas, IPEDS, NSSE, Tinto or the WICHE
  projections. They know them. Explaining them insults the reader.
- **Loop and Alma are pre-launch with no live customers.** Never imply a displacement or a
  reference customer.
- Approved sources: WICHE, NCES, IPEDS, NSSE, NACADA, EAB, EDUCAUSE, the Chronicle, Inside Higher
  Ed, the NASPA Journal, Tinto.

## Two-sided pitch

| Audience | Angle |
|---|---|
| **Platform owners** | Native to Education Cloud, not integrated beside it. No new infrastructure, no security review, SIS-agnostic, faster implementation |
| **Practitioners** | Better student outcomes, early intervention, one complete view instead of three tabs, less tool-switching |

**The anchor insight**, from the JTBD toolkit — importance 9, satisfaction 2, opportunity score
16, the highest in the map:

> Minimize the time between a student's first disengagement signal and the point when a staff
> member can act on it.

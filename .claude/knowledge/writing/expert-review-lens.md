# The expert review lens

What a subject matter expert in our market will challenge before the piece reaches them, written
down so the piece answers it first.

**Built 2026-10-05 from a full sweep of Sunil Jith's reviews**: every Doc comment, review email,
revision sheet and Chat note on the housing blog drafts from 24 August to 29 September 2026. Nine
pieces, 44 distinct revision points, plus an 8-item publishing checklist and 4 stat corrections on
the pre-NAEH content plan. Kiki's own review comments from the same drafts are folded in where they
make the same point.

**Who loads this.** Charlie, before writing a `Description` and before passing any draft on. Wrighter,
before drafting and again before delivering. `pov-editor`, at the premise check. It is shared on
purpose. Most of these defects were introduced before a sentence was written, in the angle, so a rule
held only by the writer arrives too late. See `../knowledge-transfer.md`.

---

## The governing line

Sunil, on the coordinated entry piece, 23 September:

> *"The goal is not to soften the article. It is to make the argument sharper and much harder for a
> CoC or HMIS expert to challenge."*

**Sharp thesis, narrow claims.** These rules are not a hedge. Wrighter's Thought Leadership rule,
*never soften the piece into a balanced explainer*, still stands. The thesis stays as hard as it was.
What narrows is each supporting claim, until every one survives a reader who runs the system in
question.

The reader to write for is **the HMIS administrator or CoC lead who knows the regulation better than
we do and is looking for the sentence that lets them dismiss the rest.** Sunil's phrase for what
that reader finds is *"someone will call it spin."* One overreach is enough, because the reader
generalises from it to the whole piece and to CUBE84.

**The cost of skipping this, measured.** Four pieces went to v3 or v4 on review rounds that these
tests would have caught at brief time. On two of them Sunil stopped commenting and rewrote the piece
himself, and on 12 September he wrote: *"There is a serious gap in the way your Claude works vs mine."*

## The twelve tests

Ordered by how often they fired. The count is the number of Sunil's revision points that each test
would have pre-empted. One point can fire more than one test, so the counts add up to more than 44.

### 1. The claim is no wider than the evidence · 10 points

No `most`, `every`, `nobody`, `nothing`, `a large share` or `in a lot of` unless a source counts it.
Name the scope you can actually support, and say it plainly.

- **Separate what is required from what exists.** "HUD's standards have no required element for X"
  is provable. "Your HMIS cannot do X" is not, because vendors build beyond the standard. *"The
  evidence shows HUD's data standards do not require vacancy… It does not show that no HMIS product
  can hold them."* (revision sheet, 29 Sep)
- **Our own counts show that a pattern exists, never how common it is.** Four or five of about 85 booth
  visitors is roughly 5% of a self-selected room. *"Either add a figure from an outside source, or use
  the count only to show the pattern exists and let the quotes and the rule carry the argument."*
- **Make a universal claim conditional.** Write "in communities where no structured external referral
  route exists", not "libraries and hospitals have no referral route".
- **A title or heading is still a claim.** "The Findings Are Gone by Morning" became "What Happens to
  the Findings by Morning?" so that a CoC that already debriefs keeps reading.

**Test:** circle every quantifier and every universal in the draft. Write the source beside each one.
Any that has no source narrows or goes.

### 2. Read the whole rule, and cite the paragraph that says it · 8 points

An expert reader holds the regulation open beside the piece.

- **Never quote the one line that helps.** The 578.57 argument set the software line against six
  salary activities and left out hardware, technical support and office space from the same paragraph.
  *"The 578.57 argument is selective."* Read the full paragraph or section, then describe it fairly.
- **Cite the right paragraph.** 578.7(b) is HMIS designation. Coordinated entry is 578.7(a)(8) plus
  CPD-17-01. A wrong cite tells the expert we have not read the rule.
- **Keep a restriction to its real scope.** 576.400(f) concerns comparable-database and VSP data. The
  VSP prohibition applies to organisations that qualify as Victim Service Providers, not to every
  organisation running a DV program.
- **Use the practitioner's sequence and definitions.** HUD's site lists access, assessment, referral,
  prioritization. Practitioners work access → assessment → prioritization → referral. Quote HUD
  verbatim where you quote it, and use the working order in your own prose. Coordinated entry's
  definition is Sunil's (see *Reference definitions* below).
- **A hand count that carries a chart gets re-verified against the primary document**, not its
  contents page, before the piece goes to review.

### 3. Check the premise against how the system was actually designed · 4 points

Before arguing that a system fails at X, confirm it was meant to do X.

- Coordinated entry was never one front door. HUD explicitly allows multiple access points, no wrong
  door, phone, virtual and outreach. A piece built on "CE designed one door" argues against a design
  that does not exist, and *"Sunil rejected its premise."*
- Double entry in the compliance piece came from a missing integration, not a missing schema. *"It
  states a missing schema but then that is not the reason for entering the data twice."*
- **The fix is usually the stronger thesis.** "Uninstrumented handoff against instrumented handoff" is
  sharper than "one door against many".

`pov-editor` runs this test at the consensus check. Wrighter runs it again on every causal claim.

### 4. Name what already exists, then the precise gap · 6 points

The expert's first reaction is *"we already have that"*. Say it for them.

- **Open on the concession.** Sunil's own rewrite starts its first section: *"Start with the
  concession, because it is the first thing an HMIS administrator will say."* It then grants that the
  standards are a reporting minimum, that vendors add fields and that several ship bed modules.
- **Acknowledge existing workflows and queues** to show we know the ecosystem, then say exactly which
  part is still unsolved.
- **Defend current practice before pivoting.** The PIT piece keeps its section defending paper and the
  prescribed method. *"It is what keeps the piece from reading as technology for its own sake."*
- **Expect pushback on sore points.** *"Unrestricted funding exists"* drew *"This point may get a
  push back because funding is a big problem in this sector."*
- **Validate that the gap is real before publishing.** If a representative sample of CoC policies and
  major platforms has not been checked, the gap is a hypothesis. Charlie owns that sample (see
  *At brief time*).

### 5. Answer every objection you raise · 2 points

A steelman that is never answered hands the reader the argument. *"Your own opposing case says boards
can't hire, and the piece never answers that."* The fix was a practical section: cross-training,
runbooks, pooled staff. **An objection that matters gets an answer the reader can act on.**

### 6. No exhibit is allowed to mislead · 3 points

A chart is the most quotable claim in the piece and gets the hardest look.

- Leave out a bar that only exists to look dramatic. *"The zero-width software bar is misleading… Someone will
  call it spin."*
- A figure that repeats a point already made gets cut (Figure 2 on the referral piece).
- **A caption names the source and the one thing to notice.** It does not narrate the chart. *"It
  reads more like a chart description, which can be removed."*

This tightens Rule 0 in `CLAUDE.md`. A visual must carry an insight, and **the insight has to be fair.**

### 7. A commercial close that is soft, present and verified · 4 points

Two failures, opposite to each other:

- **No close at all.** *"A vendor telling readers not to swap platforms leaves them wondering what
  you're selling."* One soft line that points at a complementary layer or a next step.
- **A forced pitch.** *"Do not turn the ending into a product pitch. Thought leadership first."*

So the close is **a CUBE84 point of view or next step**, for example *"assess how much of your
first-contact network is actually measurable"*. A product appears only when it is connected to the
gap the piece described. **Every capability claim must be confirmed live by the product team**, with
the date. Roadmap items stay out. Example: bed holds were roadmap on 29 Sep. If we don't know whether
CUBE84 offers a service, it goes in as an open question, not as a sentence.

### 8. Framework first, takeaway early, density cut · 6 points

- **Put the strongest framework near the top** and let it organise the piece. *"Mandated, Designated
  and Chosen is probably the strongest part of the article. Introduce this earlier."*
- **Bring the practical value forward** and shorten the regulatory build-up around it.
- **Keep one citation per claim.** Delete repeated evidence. On the referral piece that came to about
  20% of the length.
- **Disclosures run short.** A whole section explaining that one conversation is not a pattern
  *"reduces the authority of the piece"*. Three sentences do the job.
- **Length.** Thought Leadership carrying regulatory evidence lands at **1,600 to 1,800 words**
  (Sunil, funders piece). The board's documented default is 1,200 to 1,500. Charlie writes the real
  target into `Word Count`. A blank field is still a question for Kiki.

### 9. One named reader · 2 points

*"Who is this aimed for?"* The piece that *"reads too technical"* was trying to speak to HMIS
administrators and CoC leadership at once. Pick one, put it in the byline line under the H1
("For CoC leads, coordinated entry managers and HMIS administrators"), and cut whatever only the other
reader needs.

### 10. The headline is accurate, searchable and free of the false premise · 4 points

- It must not restate the premise the piece corrects ("One Front Door").
- It must not accuse every system ("…Cannot Connect Them" became "Your HMIS Records the Referral. But
  Does It Know Which Bed Is Open?").
- **It carries the term people search for**: coordinated entry referrals, CE access points, no wrong
  door. Charlie's keyword belongs in the H1, not only in the meta.
- Wrighter's two heading tests still apply: the heading reads alone and earns the stop.

### 11. Challenge the consensus without pulling the reader down · Kiki's comments plus 1 point

Kiki on the HMIS piece: *"You have to challenge the common consensus but not by pulling them down."*
Sunil's version is a register: *"The Count Is Understandably Treated First as a Compliance Exercise."*
**Explain why the current practice is reasonable, then show what it misses.** Never cast the reader's
past decision as habit, carelessness or failure. A vivid phrase that distracts from the argument goes
too, even when it is accurate ("a bed of the right sex").

### 12. One backbone sentence the whole piece serves · 3 points

Three of Sunil's reviews ended by writing the sentence himself:

- *"Not every system in a provider's environment is equally replaceable. Understand who controls
  each system, consolidate what the organization controls, and architect around what it doesn't."*
- *"The real comparison isn't one door versus many doors. It is an uninstrumented handoff versus an
  instrumented one."*
- PIT: *"End on a stronger version of the central question."*

**When the reviewer has to write the backbone, the brief did not hold one.** Charlie writes it before
Wrighter starts. Every section either advances it or gets cut. The close restates it in its strongest
form.

## At brief time: what Charlie adds to `Description`

The `Description` is where a defect becomes cheap or expensive. These fields go in alongside the
consensus, holder and counter-claim:

| Field | What it holds | Test it pre-empts |
|---|---|---|
| Backbone | The one sentence the piece serves | 12 |
| Reader | One named role, used in the byline | 9 |
| Premise check | How the system is actually designed, with the cite | 3 |
| What already exists | The workflows, modules or practice the expert will name first | 4 |
| Gap evidence | What was sampled to show the gap is real, or "unvalidated" said plainly | 4 |
| Claim scope | What the evidence supports, for example "pattern exists, prevalence unknown" | 1 |
| Rules in play | Each regulation, with the paragraph read in full | 2 |
| Close | The point of view or next step, plus any product capability with its confirmation date | 7 |
| Search term for the H1 | The keyword that has to appear in the headline | 10 |
| Word count | The real target, never blank | 8 |

If a row cannot be filled, **say so in the row.** "Gap evidence: unvalidated, no CoC policy sample yet"
is a complete and useful answer. A plausible guess is not.

## Before a reviewer sees it: the review Charlie runs

Use the `expert-review` skill in `review` mode on every draft before its `Status` moves past `Draft
ready, not sent`. It returns findings in Sunil's own format. He chose that format and Kiki accepted
it ("the new approach works well"):

1. An overall verdict in two sentences: what is strong, and whether it ships as is.
2. The backbone sentence, written out.
3. Numbered changes, each one carrying **the problem, why an expert would challenge it, and the fix.**
4. The split: **which items must be fixed before publishing, and which are improvements.**

The aim is that the SME's review becomes a confirmation, not a rewrite.

## Before publishing: Sunil's checklist

From the 29 September revision sheet. These are publishing steps, not drafting steps. They are
recorded here so nobody has to rediscover them:

- Byline audience line present ("For …")
- Dates in US format, for example "October 1, 2025"
- Review notes, open questions and the Review Log removed from the published version
- CUBE84's label for AI-assisted content added. **The wording does not exist yet.** Kiki owns it, and
  until it exists this item is open on every piece.
- SME named on the intake row, and the SME has read the final draft

## Reference definitions an expert supplied

Use these as written. They came from the reviewer, so they will pass the reviewer.

- **Coordinated entry:** "the CoC-wide process for how people experiencing or at risk of homelessness
  access the system, get assessed with a standard tool, and are prioritized and referred to housing
  and services using consistent standards, no matter which door they enter through."
- **Coordinated entry sequence, in prose:** access, assessment, prioritization, referral.
- **What 578.57(a)(1) funds:** software or licences, computer hardware, technical support, office
  space, and salaries for operating HMIS, the last with six lettered sub-activities.

## The reference exemplar

`hmiseligibilityrulesv4.md` in Drive (`1hr0uorBGJtYzdFqLcVLuUB-JdXfGWAFyi0Wnz9clqr4`) is Sunil's own
rewrite of the eligibility piece. When a structural choice is unclear, read it. What it does:

- a concession section placed straight after the hook
- a fact-to-source table: *the fact the worker needs → where the standards put it → when it is
  true*
- three named objections stated at full strength, each answered in turn
- measurements the reader can produce without a vendor in the room
- a short FAQ that answers the expert's questions in their own words
- an author line naming his role and the products, with no pitch

# Write one sentence where every part serves the same purpose

Kiki's correction, 2026-11-02, on the Blog Listing Page Description for *Hire Remote Salesforce
Developers: A Planning Guide*. Wrighter, Charlie and Kili all keep this rule. Each agent file
carries a short version, and this is the full reasoning.

## The example

**Rejected:**
> Before you interview a remote Salesforce developer, settle who carries the work and what it really
> costs. A three-step plan for CRM and RevOps leads.

**Kiki's version:**
> A three-step plan for CRM and RevOps professionals to determine ownership and understand the true
> cost before interviewing a remote Salesforce developer.

## Why the first one fails

1. **The second sentence is a fragment.** "A three-step plan for CRM and RevOps leads" has no verb.
   Fragments are allowed in marketing copy, but here it sits apart from the first sentence. The result
   is two statements that happen to be next to each other, not one thought.
2. **The order is reversed.** It tells the reader what to do before it tells them what they are
   looking at. A reader needs to know what something is before what it helps them do.

## Why the second one works

| Phrase | Grammatical role | Job |
|---|---|---|
| A three-step plan | Main noun phrase | What it is |
| for CRM and RevOps professionals | Prepositional phrase | Who it is for |
| to determine ownership | Infinitive phrase | First objective |
| and understand the true cost | Coordinated infinitive | Second objective |
| before interviewing a remote Salesforce developer | Time phrase | When it matters |

- **Parallel construction.** "To determine ownership and understand the true cost": the two verbs
  share one "to" and are joined by "and". That is shorter and smoother, and it loses nothing.
- **"Before interviewing", not "before you interview".** Both are correct. For a descriptive line on
  a business resource, the participle is more concise and keeps the focus on the process, not the
  person.

## The sequence to follow

For headlines, listing descriptions, meta descriptions, item summaries and any one-line "what is
this" copy:

1. **What is it?** (a three-step plan)
2. **Who is it for?** (CRM and RevOps professionals)
3. **What does it help them do?** (determine ownership, understand cost)
4. **When does it matter?** (before interviewing a remote Salesforce developer)

Context, then audience, then value, then timing, in **one sentence**.

## The principle

Kiki: "The difference between writing two statements that happen to sit together and writing one
sentence where every part serves the same purpose."

It is mostly a structural and readability issue, not a grammar one. Before shipping any one-line
description, check two things. Is it one sentence? Can you point to which of the four jobs each phrase does?
If a phrase does none of them, cut it. If two sentences share one job, merge them.

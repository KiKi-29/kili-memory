# The POV spine: Higher Education

What CUBE84 argues in this market, and what it declines to argue. `pov-editor` reads this before
writing any thesis, and every thesis that ships gets appended.

Two purposes. It stops run 6 re-arguing run 2 in different words, and it stops run 6 quietly
contradicting run 2 — which is worse and harder to notice.

*candidate* (evidenced, never published, not yet ruled on by Kiki) or a *gate* (the argument's
load-bearing half is unverified). Do not present a candidate as a house position.

## Candidate positions

### C1. The money and the motion point in opposite directions
**Status: candidate. Strongest thing in the market and the most uncomfortable.**

75% of all higher-ed Closed Won sits in two accounts, and both are alumni and advancement work.
There are **zero student-engagement wins** — Loop is pre-launch with no live customers. Yet the
outbound motion, the conference investment, the ad creative and most of the persona work all aim
at student engagement. The paid record says the same from another angle: the only two higher-ed
paid-search wins in nineteen months both arrived on *staffing* intent, and across 434 paid rows
there is not one alumni, advancement or "higher education" keyword.

- **Consensus being challenged:** that student engagement is CUBE84's higher-ed wedge.
- **Who holds it:** CUBE84's own GTM motion, and the conference and outbound spend behind it.
- **Falsifiable counter-claim:** the wedge is advancement, and student engagement is a product
  looking for the market that advancement already gave us.
- **Why it is not shipped:** this is an argument about our own strategy, aimed inward. It needs
  the BU lead behind it before any of it faces outward, and probably never ships as a blog at
  all. It belongs in a room, not on the site.

### C2. Salesforce is a platform brand in higher ed, not an application brand
**Status: candidate. Verified, publishable, and genuinely useful to a buyer.**

Adding "salesforce" to a higher-ed category term destroys roughly 94% of search demand, and
retention varies sharply by layer — 11.2% at the CRM layer, 3.8% in alumni, 0.7% in admissions
where Slate owns the word. CUBE84 sells at the application layer while being findable only under
the platform.

- **Consensus:** that being a Salesforce partner is a discovery advantage in higher ed.
- **Who holds it:** every Salesforce implementation partner competing on "salesforce for X" terms.
- **Counter-claim:** the platform name is a filter, not a magnet. Buyers search the job, and the
  partner ecosystem is optimised for a query nobody types.

### C3. The person who feels the pain cannot say what the institution runs
**Status: candidate. The heaviest signal in the ledger at 8 organisations.**

Student-affairs and student-success contacts repeatedly cannot state whether their institution
runs Salesforce, or what it is used for, because the CRM is owned by IT, enrolment or advancement.
A second pattern sits on top of it at 6 organisations: the person who engages has no budget line.

- **Consensus:** that the practitioner closest to the student is the buyer for student-success
  software.
- **Who holds it:** most student-success vendors' go-to-market, and CUBE84's own abandoned
  case-manager ICP.
- **Counter-claim:** in this category the practitioner is the *convener*, not the buyer — five
  organisations offered to assemble the group rather than stalling. Asking for the group is the
  qualification step, and a motion that treats them as the buyer mistakes a convener for a
  decision maker.
- **Corroboration on record:** the case-manager ICP was abandoned 2026-06-03 after 48 connected
  conversations produced zero booked meetings.

### C4. The belonging layer lives outside the system that holds the student record
**Status: candidate. 5 organisations, plus a 4-organisation companion on the advisor view.**

Institutions have bought into the category and own three or four systems that do not talk — a CRM,
an advising platform, a co-curricular engagement app, an SIS. One runs roughly 50 applications for
1,000 students. Participation data never reaches the person advising the student.

- **Counter-claim:** replacement cycles in this category repeat without resolving anything,
  because dissatisfaction is about permissions and connectivity, not features.
- **Handle with care:** we have not displaced anyone. Loop is pre-launch. Write the mechanism,
  never the win.

## Gates — arguments whose load-bearing half is unverified

### G1. The partner channel, not paid media, is the route into advancement
Kindsight's Ascend advancement CRM is built on Salesforce and its Alliance Partner tier is defined
as firms that implement Ascend. **But** a replace-Kindsight opportunity was Closed Lost on
2026-07-06. Partner-versus-displace is an open commercial decision, and the thesis reverses
depending on which way it goes. **Do not write either side until it is decided.**

### G2. Corporate Engagement (Folio)
Unresolved whether it means universities engaging corporate partners, or companies engaging their
own former employees. Different markets, almost no keyword overlap. **No thesis, no keyword pull,
no topic until Kiki rules.**

## What this audience will not read

From `Higher Ed/ebook_Content_Writing_Guide_HigherEd.md`, which is the de facto editorial standard
for this market and was written against the real audience.

- **Buzzwords and vague claims.** They have sat in rooms where a platform was promised as the fix
  for retention and it did not fully deliver.
- **Criticism of a platform they have already implemented and defended to their board.** This is
  the sharpest rule in the market and it constrains C4 directly. Name the *mechanism* that fails,
  never the vendor or the buyer's past decision.
- **A product introduced before the problem is established.**
- They already know Navigate360, Starfish, Education Cloud, Banner, Ellucian, Canvas, Blackboard,
  IPEDS retention rates, NSSE, Tinto and the WICHE projections. Explaining these insults them.

## Approved sources for this market

WICHE, NCES, IPEDS, NSSE, NACADA, EAB, EDUCAUSE, the Chronicle of Higher Education, Inside Higher
Ed, the NASPA Journal, and Tinto (1987, 1993). Anything outside this list needs a reason.

Two sector facts already verified and citable: high-school graduates peaked in 2025 and decline
**13% through 2041** (WICHE 2024), and the college-going rate fell from **70% in 2016 to roughly
62%** today. Retention is a CFO conversation now, not only a student-affairs one.

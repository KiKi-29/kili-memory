## Design team, referred to internally as Nova

**The rule: every communication that touches design, in either direction, is handled with
care and collaborative language. No exceptions, including when you are right.**

**The reason Kili needs to know:** the working relationship is delicate. Kiki is perceived
by the design team as intimidating, and its head takes a strong protective interest in the
team's scope and ownership. That combination means a message that is merely blunt, or that
reads as checking up on them, damages Kiki's standing rather than solving the problem it
was about. The technical content can be flawless and the outcome still bad.

**How to write anything involving design:**

- Collaborative and warm. Ask, do not instruct. "Could we", not "you need to".
- Never imply the team is late, has missed something, or has made an error, even where the
  evidence is plain. Raise the situation, not the fault.
- Never phrase something as a correction of their work.
- Assume good faith out loud. If a thing was missed, the reason is process, not person.
- Where a question can be routed through the person who owns the content instead, route it
  there. That is usually the better path anyway.

**Never surface their internal boards.** Design keeps its own working boards, notably
`Daily Tracker` (`8820743652`, CoDe Team Folder). Kili may read them for her own
understanding. She must **never** cite one back to design, or to anyone in a way that
would reach design, because it reads as surveillance regardless of intent.

When something learned from a design-internal board needs acting on, **find a neutral route
to the same fact.** Ask the content owner what is planned. Ask about timelines rather than
about their work. The answer arrives without anyone feeling watched.

## What this means in practice

Before anything reaches or references the design team, ask three questions:

1. **Does this read as checking up on them?** If yes, rewrite or find another route.
2. **Am I attributing a miss to a person?** Attribute it to process instead.
3. **Could the content owner answer this instead?** Usually yes, and usually better.

If Kili cannot find a way to raise something without one of those failing, that is a case
for Kiki, not a message to send. Say so and stop.
